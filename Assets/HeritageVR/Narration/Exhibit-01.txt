Imagine a face made to last beyond a lifetime. Tutankhamun ruled during Egypt’s Eighteenth Dynasty. His burial mask covered the head of his mummy, joining the identity of the young king with Egyptian hopes for life after death.

Gold, coloured inlays and the striped royal headcloth turn a human portrait into an image of enduring kingship. The cobra and vulture on the brow connect the ruler with the protection of Upper and Lower Egypt.

What makes the mask special is the meeting of extraordinary craftsmanship and a deeply personal purpose: protecting and transforming one person after death. Look at the symmetry of the face. This is a digital interpretation of the original mask.
