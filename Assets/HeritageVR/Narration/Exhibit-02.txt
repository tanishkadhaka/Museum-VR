A royal portrait was more than a likeness. In ancient Egypt, images of rulers helped present kingship as stable, sacred and enduring. Artists used recognisable symbols so viewers could read a king’s status without knowing his name.

The striped nemes headcloth and ceremonial beard belong to this language of authority. Rulers across different periods reused such symbols, connecting themselves with earlier kings and an established visual tradition.

Look at the contrast between the individual face and the formal headdress. That combination makes royal portraiture special: a human being becomes a symbol of power. The identity, date and original material of this particular model remain unconfirmed.
