Imagine approaching a monument watched over by a powerful animal. In ancient Greek sculpture, marble lions could mark tombs or guard the ends of a tomb’s facade. The animal made protection visible in stone.

A lion’s open mouth, mane and extended paws could suggest strength even when the sculpture stood still. Such features helped a sculptor turn an animal into a lasting presence beside a place of remembrance.

What is special here is the suggestion of movement in a fixed form. Walk around the crouching body and notice how the silhouette changes. This explains a historical tradition; the exact origin and date of this model are unconfirmed.
